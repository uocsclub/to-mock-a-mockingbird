* Bluebirds and Thrushes

From a bluebird B and a thrush T, you can derive a cardinal (with 8 letters). How?

** Clue 1

Bxyz = x(yz)

From B and T, how can we derive a Robin satisfying this condition:

Rxyz = yzx  (Robin rotates its arguments)

*** Answer

yzx = (yz)x = Tx(yz) = DT = BBT.

** Clue 2

And now, from just the robin alone, we can derive a cardinal. Can you see how? The solution is quite pretty.

*** Answer

Robin rotates its arguments! We want: Cxyz = xzy = Ryxz = Ryx = RxRy = RxR = RRRx = RRR. But wait, this is 9 letters!

** Answer

C = RRR = (DT)(DT)(DT) = (BBT)(BBT)(BBT) = B(T(BBT))(BBT)

* Two Useful Laws

Prove that for any bird x, the following facts hold:

a. Cx = RxR
b. Cx = B(Tx)R

** Answer

Cx = RxR: Cxyz = xzy, so we want to show that RxRyz = xyz. RxRyz = Ryxz = xzy.

Cx = B(Tx)R: B(Tx)Ryz = (Tx)(Ry)z = Ryxz = xzy.

* A Question

You have just seen that a cardinal can be derived from a robin. Can a robin be derived from a cardinal?

** Answer

We want Rxyz = Cyxz = Cyx = CC.

* Finches

Fxyz = zyx  (reverses)

A finch can be easily derived from a bluebird, a robin, and a cardinal—and hence from a bluebird and a robin or from a bluebird and a cardinal. Can you see how?

** Answer

zyx = Rxzy = C(Rx)zy = BCR

with R = CC: BC(CC)
with C = RRR: B(RRR)R

zyx = Rxzy = R(Cxyz) = B₂RC = B(BBB)BRC

* More finch

Alternatively, a finch can be derived from a thrush T and an eagle E. Can you see how?

** Answer

zyx = Tx(zy) = Tx(Tyz) = ETxT = TT(ETx) = ETTET

* More finch cont'd

Now you have two methods of expressing a finch in terms of a bluebird B and a thrush T. You will see that one of them yields a much shorter expression than the other.

Recall:
R = BBT
C = B(T(BBT))(BBT)
E = B(BBB)

** Answer

F = BCR = B(B(T(BBT))(BBT))(BBT)
F = ETTET = B(BBB)TT(B(BBB))T = B(TT)(B(BBB)T)
