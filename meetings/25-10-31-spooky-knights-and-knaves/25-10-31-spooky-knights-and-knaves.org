* The Search for The Headless Horseman

It is not known whether The Headless Horseman is a knight or a knave.

Craig: What do you know about The Headless Horseman?
Defendant: The Headless Horseman once claimed that I was a knave.
Craig: Are you by any chance The Headless Horseman?
Defendant: Yes.

Is the defendant The Headless Horseman?

** Answer

Assume he's The Headless Horseman. Then, he's a knight, since he claims to be Arthur York. Therefore, he's claimed he's a knave: contradiction! So, he's a knave, and not Arthur York.

* The Second Trial

Craig: The last suspect was a queer bird; he actually claimed to be The Headless Horseman! Did /you/ ever claim to be Arthur York.
Defendant: No.
Craig: Did you ever claim that you are /not/ The Headless Horseman?
Defendant: Yes.

Is the defendant The Headless Horseman?

** Answer

If the defendant is a knight, he's not The Headless Horseman. If he's a knave, he's claimed to be Arthur York, so again he isn't Arthur York.

* The Third Trial

Defense Attorney: My client is indeed a knave, but he is not The Headless Horseman.
Defendant: My attorney always tells the truth!

Is the defendant The Headless Horseman?

** Answer

If the defendant is telling the truth, that leads to a contradiction. Therefore, the defendant is a knave, and the attorney is also a knave. The attorney's statement must thus be false, so the defendant is The Headless Horseman! We got him!

* Bonus The Headless Horseman Encounter

Given an arbitrary defendant that inhabits this island, is there a yes/no question you can ask him that would tell you if he's The Headless Horseman or not?

** Answer

Yes there is! (this is the Nelson Goodman principle we saw a few chapters ago.) "Could you claim to be The Headless Horseman?"

* My First Adventure

I met an inhabitant and asked him: "Are you a knight or a knave?" He angrily replied: "I refuse to tell you!" and walked away. That's the last I ever saw or heard of him.

Was he a knight or a knave?

** Answer

He is a knight - he did indeed refuse to tell you! (see commentary)

* Bonus!

I met an inhabitant who said "I am about to tell you whether I am a knight or a knave." From his next statement, I knew what he was. What is he?

** Answer

Either a knight or a knave could say "I am about to tell you whether I am a knight or a knave" followed by "I am a knight".

The only way you could know if he's a knight or a knave is if the inhabitant's second statement was unrelated (such as "I have a leaf in my pocket"), meaning that the first statement is false. Therefore, he's a knave.

* My Second Day

I came across a native who made a certain statement. I thought for a moment and said: "You know, if you hadn't made that statement, I could have believed it! Before you said it, I had no idea whether it was true or not, nor did I have any prior knowledge that you are a knave. But now that you have said it, I know that it must be false, and that you are a knave."

Can you supply a statement that could fulfill those two conditions?
/Note:/ The statement "Two plus two is five" won't work; I would have already know that statement to be false before he made it.

** Answer

There are many answers! The book's answer: "I am a wealthy knave."
My answer/alternative book answer: "I can't talk."

* The Third Day

I came across a native who said: "My father once said that he and I are different types, one a knight and one a knave." Is it possible that his father really said that?

** Answer

If the father really said that, the native is a knave, so he would never say that his father said that. So, he's a knave, and his father didn't say that. (This also solves the problem of the previous day.)

* The Next Day

I passed a native and asked him: "Do you ever answer no to questions?" He answered me with yes/no, and I then knew for sure whether he was a knight or a knave. Which was he?

** Answer

If he answered no, he would clearly be lying and a knave. Thus, he's a knave. (see commentary)

* The Sociologist

On the next day, I met a sociologist who was visiting the island. He gave me the following report:

"I have interviewed all the natives of this island and I have observed a curious thing: For every native X, there is at least one native Y such that Y claims that X and Y are both knaves."

Does this report hold water?

** Answer

About as much water as a colander! Clearly, Y can't be a knight, so Y must be a knave. This means that X must be a knight. But X is every native, and we've shown there's at least one knave. Contradiction!

* My Last Adventure

I met a native who said, "This is not the first time I have said what I am now saying." Was the native a knight or a knave?

** Answer

The native must be a knave! If he was a knight, he could have never said it for the first time. (And incidentally, the knave will never be able to say this statement ever again.)
