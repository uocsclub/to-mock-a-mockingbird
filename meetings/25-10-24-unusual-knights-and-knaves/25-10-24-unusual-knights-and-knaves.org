* A Metapuzzle (what's that?)

Arthur is an accurate truth-teller, Bernard is an inaccurate truth-teller, Charles is an accurate liar, David is an inaccurate liar.

One day a logician came across one of the four brothers and asked him, "Who are you?" The brother identified himself as either Arthur, Bernard, Charles, or David, and the logician then knew who he was.

A few minutes later, the same brother was met by a second logician, who asked him, "Who do you believe you are?" The brother answered, again either Arthur, Bernard, Charles, or David, and the second logician then knew who he was.

Who is he?

** Answer

Remember, A and D always respond accurately, while B and C respond inaccurately. Therefore, A, B, or C could all claim to be A; same with B, C, D claiming D. Therefore, the brother is either Bernard or Charles.

When questions about belief are asked, they respond according to their honesty. So, A and B would respond with A and B respectively, while C and D would respond with something that isn't C or D respectively. Therefore, the brother responded David or Charles, and is either Charles or David.

∴ the brother is Charles.

* The Mystery of the Photograph

Arthur is an accurate truth-teller, Bernard is an inaccurate truth-teller, Charles is an accurate liar, David is an inaccurate liar.

If you ever visit these four brothers at their home, you will notice a photograph of one of them in the living room. If you ask each of them whether it is /his/ photograph, three of them will answer no and one will answer yes. If you ask each one whether he /believes/ it is his photograph, then again three will answer no and one will answer yes.

Whose photograph is it?

** Answer

"Is it your photograph?" 3n, 1y:
it's Bernard or Charles's photograph

"Do you /believe/ it is your photograph?":
If it's Bernard's, N Y Y Y
If it's Charles's, N N N Y

So, again, the answer is Charles!

But, does it even matter?

* Refresher: knights and knaves

On a certain island, each inhabitant is either a knight or a knaves. Knights make only true statements, and knaves make only false ones.

1. Is it possible for any inhabitant to claim that he is a knave?
2. Is it possible for an inhabitant of the island to claim that he and his brother are both knaves?
3. Suppose an inhabitant A says about himself and his brother B: "At least one of us is a knave." What type is A and what type is B?

** Answers

1. Nope!
2. Yep, if he is a knave and his brother is a knight. (see commentary in book)
3. A can't be a knave since he would then be telling the truth, so A is a knight and B is a knave.

* More refreshments

4. Suppose A instead says "Exactly one of us is a knave." What can be deduced about A and what can be deduced about B?
5. Suppose A instead says: "My brother and I are the same type." What can be deduced about A and B? Suppose A had instead said "My brother and I are different types." What can then be deduced?

** Answers

4. Either A is a knight and B is a knave, or they're both knaves. So we know nothing about A, but B is a knave.
5. If A says they're both the same type, B couldn't be a knave, since that would be the same as A claiming to be a knave, which is impossible.
   If A says they're both different types, B can't be a knight, since again that's A claiming to be a knave (this is the same as 4.).

* The Search for Arthur York

It is not known whether Arthur York is a knight or a knave.

Craig: What do you know about Arthur York?
Defendant: Arthur York once claimed that I was a knave.
Craig: Are you by any chance Arthur York?
Defendant: Yes.

Is the defendant Arthur York?

** Answer

Assume he's Arthur York. Then, he's a knight, since he claims to be Arthur York. Therefore, he's claimed he's a knave: contradiction! So, he's a knave, and not Arthur York.

* The Second Trial

Craig: The last suspect was a queer bird; he actually claimed to be Arthur York! Did /you/ ever claim to be Arthur York.
Defendant: No.
Craig: Did you ever claim that you are /not/ Arthur York?
Defendant: Yes.

Is the defendant Arthur York?

** Answer

If the defendant is a knight, he's not Arthur York. If he's a knave, he's claimed to be Arthur York, so again he isn't Arthur York.

* The Third Trial

Defense Attorney: My client is indeed a knave, but he is not Arthur York.
Defendant: My attorney always tells the truth!

Is the defendant Arthur York?

** Answer

If the defendant is telling the truth, that leads to a contradiction. Therefore, the defendant is a knave, and the attorney is also a knave. The attorney's statement must thus be false, so the defendant is Arthur York! We got him!

* Bonus Arthur York Encounter

Given an arbitrary defendant that inhabits this island, is there a yes/no question you can ask him that would tell you if he's Arthur York or not?

** Answer

Yes there is! (this is the Nelson Goodman principle we saw a few chapters ago.) "Could you claim to be Arthur York?"

* My First Adventure

I met an inhabitant and asked him: "Are you a knight or a knave?" He angrily replied: "I refuse to tell you!" and walked away. That's the last I ever saw or heard of him.

Was he a knight or a knave?

** Answer

He is a knight - he did indeed refuse to tell you! (see commentary)

* My Second Day

I came across a native who made a certain statement. I thought for a moment and said: "You know, if you hadn't made that statement, I could have believed it! Before you said it, I had no idea whether it was true or not, nor did I have any prior knowledge that you are a knave. But now that you have said it, I know that it must be false, and that you are a knave."

Can you supply a statement that could fulfill those two conditions?
/Note:/ The statement "Two plus two is five" won't work; I would have already know that statement to be false before he made it.

** Answer

There are many answers! The book's answer: "I am a wealthy knave."
My answer/alternative book answer: "I can't talk."

* The Third Day

I came across a native who said: "My father once said that he and I are different types, one a knight and one a knave." Is it possible that his father really said that?

** Answer

If the father really said that, the native is a knave, so he would never say that his father said that. So, he's a knave, and his father didn't say that. (This also solves the problem of the previous day.)

* The Next Day

I passed a native and asked him: "Do you ever answer no to questions?" He answered me with yes/no, and I then knew for sure whether he was a knight or a knave. Which was he?

** Answer

If he answered no, he would clearly be lying and a knave. Thus, he's a knave. (see commentary)

* The Sociologist

On the next day, I met a sociologist who was visiting the island. He gave me the following report:

"I have interviewed all the natives of this island and I have observed a curious thing: For every native X, there is at least one native Y such that Y claims that X and Y are both knaves."

Does this report hold water?

** Answer

About as much water as a colander! Clearly, Y can't be a knight, so Y must be a knave. This means that X must be a knight. But X is every native, and we've shown there's at least one knave. Contradiction!

* My Last Adventure

I met a native who said, "This is not the first time I have said what I am now saying." Was the native a knight or a knave?

** Answer

The native must be a knave! If he was a knight, he could have never said it for the first time. (And incidentally, the knave will never be able to say this statement ever again.)
