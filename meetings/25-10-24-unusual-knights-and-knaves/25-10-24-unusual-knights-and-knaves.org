* A Metapuzzle (what's that?)

Arthur is an accurate truth-teller, Bernard is an inaccurate truth-teller, Charles is an accurate liar, David is an inaccurate liar.

One day a logician asks one of the four brothers, "Who are you?" The brother identified himself as one of the four, and the logician then knew who he was.

A few minutes later, the same brother was met by a second logician, who asked him, "Who do you believe you are?" The brother answered, again with one of the four names, and the second logician then knew who he was.

Who is he?

** Answer

Remember, A and D always respond accurately, while B and C respond inaccurately. Therefore, A, B, or C could all claim to be A; same with B, C, D claiming D. Therefore, the brother is either Bernard or Charles.

When questions about belief are asked, they respond according to their honesty. So, A and B would respond with A and B respectively, while C and D would respond with something that isn't C or D respectively. Therefore, the brother responded David or Charles, and is either Charles or David.

∴ the brother is Charles.

* The Mystery of the Photograph

Arthur is an accurate truth-teller, Bernard is an inaccurate truth-teller, Charles is an accurate liar, David is an inaccurate liar.

If you ever visit these four brothers at their home, you will notice a photograph of one of them in the living room. If you ask each of them whether it is /his/ photograph, three of them will answer no and one will answer yes. If you ask each one whether he /believes/ it is his photograph, then again three will answer no and one will answer yes.

Whose photograph is it?

** Answer

"Is it your photograph?" 3n, 1y:
it's Bernard or Charles's photograph

"Do you /believe/ it is your photograph?":
If it's Bernard's, N Y Y Y
If it's Charles's, N N N Y

So, again, the answer is Charles!

But, does it even matter?

* Refresher: knights and knaves

On a certain island, each inhabitant is either a knight or a knaves. Knights make only true statements, and knaves make only false ones.

1. Is it possible for any inhabitant to claim that he is a knave?
2. Is it possible for an inhabitant of the island to claim that he and his brother are both knaves?
3. Suppose an inhabitant A says about himself and his brother B: "At least one of us is a knave." What type is A and what type is B?

** Answers

1. Nope!
2. Yep, if he is a knave and his brother is a knight. (see commentary in book)
3. A can't be a knave since he would then be telling the truth, so A is a knight and B is a knave.

* More refreshments

4. Suppose A instead says "Exactly one of us is a knave." What can be deduced about A and what can be deduced about B?
5. Suppose A instead says: "My brother and I are the same type." What can be deduced about A and B? Suppose A had instead said "My brother and I are different types." What can then be deduced?

** Answers

4. Either A is a knight and B is a knave, or they're both knaves. So we know nothing about A, but B is a knave.
5. If A says they're both the same type, B couldn't be a knave, since that would be the same as A claiming to be a knave, which is impossible.
   If A says they're both different types, B can't be a knight, since again that's A claiming to be a knave (this is the same as 4.).
