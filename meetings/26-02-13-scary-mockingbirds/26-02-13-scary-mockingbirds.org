* A Question on Agreeable Birds

Suppose that the composition condition of Problem 1 holds and that A, B, and C are birds such that C composes A with B. Prove that if C is agreeable then A is also agreeable.

** Answer

Suppose that C is agreeable. Take any bird D. So there exists a bird E that composes D with B (in the future, I'm going to write composition birds E like D∘B to reduce the amount of letters I use). There exists a bird x on which C and E agree. So Cx = Ex. But Cx = A(Bx) and Ex = D(Bx). So A and D agree on Bx, and A is agreeable.

* An Exercise in Composition

Again suppose that the composition condition holds. Prove that for any birds A, B, and C there is a bird D such that for every bird x, Dx = A(B(Cx)). This fact is quite useful.

** Answer

Since B and C are birds, B∘C is also a bird. Since A and B∘C are birds, A∘(B∘C) are also birds. And for all birds x, (A∘(B∘C))x = A((B∘C)x) = A(B(Cx)). So D = A∘(B∘C).

* Compatible Birds

Two birds A and B, either the same or different, are called /compatible/ if there is a bird x and a bird y, either the same or different such that Ax = y and By = x. This means that if you call out x to A then you will get y as a response, whereas if you call out y to B, you will get x as a response.

Prove that if both conditions of Problem 1 (the composition condition and the mockingbird condition) hold, then any two birds A and B are compatible.

** Answer

Since, under the conditions of problem 1, every bird is fond of another, A∘B is fond of an x. So A(Bx) = x. Let y = Bx. Thus Ay = x and Bx = y, as desired.

Now, if we do this the other way around (like B∘A), do we get another pair that makes A and B compatible? From problem 1, we actually /know/ the x A∘B is fond of: x = (A∘B∘M)(A∘B∘M) = (A∘B)((A∘B∘M)(A∘B∘M)) = (A∘B)x. So y = B[(A∘B∘M)(A∘B∘M)] (I'm just using square brackets as another type, it's the same as round); Bx = y and Ay = x. Now, if we consider B∘A, x' = (B∘A∘M)(B∘A∘M) and y' = A[(B∘A∘M)(B∘A∘M)]; Ax' = y' and By' = x'. So unless A∘B = B∘A, then there are actually /two/ pairs where A and B are compatible!

* Frightened Birds

A Bird A is called /frightened/ if it is compatible with itself. This means that there are birds x and y such that Ax = y and Ay = x.

Prove that any bird that is fond of at least one bird must be a frightened bird. (Note: no conditions are assumed here.)

** Answer

Assume A is fond of x. So Ax = x. Taking y = x, Ax = y and Ay = x.

* Normal Birds

We will henceforth call a bird /normal/ if it is fond of at least one bird. We have just proved that every normal bird is frightened. The converse is not necessarily true; a frightened bird is not necessarily normal.

Prove that if the composition condition holds and if there is at least one frightened bird in the forest, then there is at least one normal bird.

** Answer

Let's assume there's a frightened bird. So there's a bird B and two birds x and y where Bx = y and By = x. (B∘B)(x) = B(Bx) = By = x. So B∘B is normal! In fact, we can prove something stronger: either there is a bird that is fond of at least two distinct birds, or there are at least two different frightened birds, or there's a projection bird (ie a bird P where P∘P = P).
