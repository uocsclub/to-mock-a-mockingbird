* Egocentric?

A bird x is called /egocentric/ if it is fond of itself. In symbols, x is egocentric if xx = x.

Prove, under the two conditions of the last problem, that at least one bird is egocentric.

* Story of the Agreeable Bird

A and B are said to /agree/ on a bird x if Ax = Bx. A bird A is called /agreeable/ if for every bird B, there is at least one bird x on which A and B agree.

We are given the composition condition, but we are not given that there is a mockingbird; instead, we are given that there is an agreeable bird A. Is this enough to guarantee that every bird is fond of at least one bird?

** Bonus question

Why was the first question no more than a special case of this one?

* A Question on Agreeable Birds

Suppose that the composition of Problem 1 holds and that A, B, and C are birds such that C composes A with B. Prove that if C is agreeable then A is also agreeable.

* An Exercise in Competition

Again suppose that the composition condition holds. Prove that for any birds A, B, and C there is a bird D such that for every bird x, Dx = A(B(Cx)). This fact is quite useful.

* Compatible Birds

Two birds A and B, either the same or different, are called /compatible/ if there is a bird x and a bird y, either the same or different such that Ax = y and By = x. This means that if you call out x to A then you will get y as a response, whereas if you call out y to B, you will get x as a response.

Prove that if both conditions of Problem 1 (the composition condition and the mockingbird condition) hold, then any two birds A and B are compatible.

* Happy Birds

A Bird A is called /happy/ if it is compatible with itself. This means that there are birds x and y such that Ax = y and Ay = x.

Prove that any bird that is fond of at least one bird must be a happy bird. (Note: no conditions are assumed here.)

* Normal Birds

We will henceforth call a bird /normal/ if it is fond of at least one bird. We have just proved that every normal bird is happy. The converse is not necessarily true; a happy bird is not necessarily normal.

Prove that if the composition condition holds and if there is at least one happy bird in the forest, then there is at least one normal bird.
