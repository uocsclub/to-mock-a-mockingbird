* Liars that... don't lie?

(See book for more context) suppose we have two brothers. One of them is completely accurately in his beliefs, and always tells the truth. The other is completely mistaken in his beliefs, and always lies. It thus appears that both brothers will always answer the same. Is it possible to tell them apart?

** Answer

It is! You can ask "do you lie".

Note that this is only possible because lying means "going against one's beliefs", not "saying something untrue".

* I thought we were done with brothers...

We have four brothers that are identical quadruplets:

**Arthur** is an accurate truth-teller
**Bernard** is an inaccurate truth-teller
**Charles** is an accurate liar
**David** is an inaccurate liar

Arthur and David will give correct answers, whereas both Bernard and Charles will give incorrect answers.

Suppose you meet one of the brothers. You wish to find out his first name, and are only allowed to ask yes/no questions. What is the smallest number of questions you need ask, and what would the questions be?

** Answer

2 questions: "do you lie?", "are you accurate in your beliefs?". Could also ask "Does 2 + 2 equal 4?", then "are you X".

* The second quadruplet problem

Arthur is an accurate truth-teller, Bernard is an inaccurate truth-teller, Charles is an accurate liar, David is an inaccurate liar.

Arthur and Bernard are both married; the other two brothers are not. Arthur and Charles are both wealthy; the other two are not.

You meet one of the four brothers one day and wish to find out whether he is married. What yes/no question would you ask? This can be done with a three-word question! What if you would like to figure out it he's wealthy?

** Answer

"Are you wealthy?"/"Are you married?" See the previous Arthur/Teresa/Lenore questions and Emilien's triangle.

* Pointless

Arthur is an accurate truth-teller, Bernard is an inaccurate truth-teller, Charles is an accurate liar, David is an inaccurate liar.

I once met one of the four brothers and asked him a yes/no question. I should have realized before I asked it that the question was pointless, because I could have known in advance what the answer would be. Can you supply such a question?

** Answer

"Are you Arthur or David?" Arthur and David, responding correctly, will answer yes; likewise for Bernard and Charles responding incorrectly.

* Place your bets

Arthur is an accurate truth-teller, Bernard is an inaccurate truth-teller, Charles is an accurate liar, David is an inaccurate liar.

Suppose someone makes you the following offer. You are to interview one of the four brothers and try to find out which one he is. You may ask him one question, or you may ask him two questions, but you must decide in advance which you will do. If you choose the two-question option and ascertain his identity, you will be given a prize of a hundred dollars. But if you choose the one-question option and can determine his identity, you will get a thousand dollars. However, under this option, if you fail after the first question, you are not allowed to ask a second.

From the point of view of pure mathematical probability, would you choose the one-question option or the two-question option?

** Answer

With only one question, you have a 50% chance of getting the brother. Expected profit: ($1000)(50%) = $500.
With two questions, you can always get the $100.

* Who is it?

Arthur is an accurate truth-teller, Bernard is an inaccurate truth-teller, Charles is an accurate liar, David is an inaccurate liar.

Suppose you ask one of the brothers whether two plus two equals four and he answers no. Then you ask him whether he /believes/ that two plus two equals four and he answers yes. Which of the four brothers is it?

** Answer

Since he says that 2 + 2 ≠ 4, he's Bernard or Charles. Since he says he /believes/ that two plus two equals four, he's Arthur or Bernard. Therefore, he's Bernard.

Appendix:

Suppose you ask each brother "Do you believe that two plus two equals four?" What will each brother answer?


Arthur: Yes
Bernard: Yes
Charles: No
David: No

Truth-tellers answer yes, while liars answer no.
